Lab costs and teardown

verifiedReviewed 2026-11-09Source: azure/lab-costs.md

A lab run ends with the environment torn down, or with everything deallocated that can be. Stopping the VMs isn't enough on its own.

  • Deallocate, don't just stop. A VM stopped from inside the guest keeps its compute reserved and billed. Stop it from Azure so it's deallocated (states and billing). Disks and networking keep billing either way.
  • Some resources bill while the VMs are off. A NAT Gateway, Azure Bastion Basic or higher, public IPs, private endpoints and managed disks keep billing whatever the VMs do. Moreover, the landing zone's budget Lock only deallocates session hosts—it does not stop the ProfileUnity console, the FlexApp packaging VM, or the Stratusphere Hub. Bastion Developer is the exception: no hourly or outbound data charge, but one connection at a time and only to VMs in its own VNet (SKU comparison). Tearing the environment down is the only way to stop those.
  • Key Vault after a teardown. With purge protection the vault stays soft-deleted for its retention period, and its name can't be reused until it's recovered or purged. The repo's deploy script recovers it.
  • Cheaper console access. One small desktop peered to the assessment VNet can replace Bastion as the way into the consoles. Deallocate it between sittings. The landing zone's opt-in jumpbox (avd-landing-zone decision 0019) is a Linux VM in the spoke reached through Bastion Developer. Bastion Developer itself does not follow peering, so it can't reach VMs in a peered assessment VNet directly.

Prices change; Get-LwCostEstimate -QueryRetailApi queries prices.azure.com for the target region before any live run, or check the Azure pricing calculator rather than recording static figures in prose.
